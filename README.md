# PhantomSDR-Plus WebSDR - Archive

**Maintained by SV1BTL.** The current version is 5.5.0, and the project lives at
**https://github.com/sv1btl/PhantomSDR-Plus** — that is the one to clone, link to
and report issues against.

🌐 **Website: [phantomsdr.psychomed.gr](https://phantomsdr.psychomed.gr)** — what PhantomSDR-Plus does, the decoders, the Desktop PhantomSDR+ app, and the documentation PDFs in seven languages.

🎬 **Video tutorial: [PhantomSDR-Plus 5.4.0 — Complete Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R5UzdGZilcQ)** — every function of the receiver in 18 minutes, recorded live on the SV1BTL station, with subtitles in English, Ελληνικά, Deutsch, Français, Español, Русский and Hrvatski.

## Note: Tested on Debian 12 (Bookworm), Debian 13 (Trixie), Ubuntu 22.04, Ubuntu 24.04.

**What's new in v.5.5.0**

* **A volume slider that goes all the way down.** 0 % is now real silence (it used to stay audible), and below the middle the slider fades out smoothly with fine steps at low levels; from 50 % up it sounds exactly as before. The slider remembers where each listener left it, on the desktop page and on /mobile.
* **A start volume for each receiver.** The sysop sets the volume a first-time listener starts at — **AT VOLUME** on the admin Receivers page, or `volume = 10` under `[input.defaults]` in the receiver's config; without it the page starts at 50 %. A returning listener keeps their own setting.
* **Receivers page in the admin panel.** **CHANGE SETTINGS** on every receiver: band, bandwidth, start frequency, mode and button name on extra receivers; button name, start frequency and mode on the main and direct-sampling ones; a change of name alone restarts nothing. The receiver's **gain** is in the same form — RX888 VGA gain, low/high mode, attenuation and PGA, RTL-SDR RF gain or auto — kept in `instances/<id>/instance.env`, or in the new `main.env` for the main receiver, which every `start-*.sh` reads and `update.sh` never overwrites. **STOP / START** for each receiver, **RENAME BUTTON**, and **DELETE** for extra receivers (with a type-the-name confirmation; its folder goes to `admin-backups/`). `proxy.py` picks up a changed `receivers.toml` by itself, and the listener pages see a restarted receiver within 15 s. See [docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md](docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md).
* **The admin panel on a phone or tablet.** The menu opens from **MENU** in the bottom bar, tables turn into one card per row and forms into one column, so every page can be used on a small screen.
* **Settings shows the station's ports** — every port in use, whether it is listening and which ones the router has to forward — and the first-run wizard asks only for the admin password.
* **Fixes.** The admin panel, `proxy.py`, `add-receiver.sh`, `start-all.sh` and `configure-station.sh` read their TOML files on Python 3.10 too (Receivers → Add gave HTTP 500 there); a receiver started by systemd at boot now finds Node installed through nvm, so DRM works after a reboot; the installers name the RADE V2 (beta) step next to RADE V1; **Mute** now silences decoded RADE V1 and V2 speech too, as it already did DRM; and **IQ** now feeds Dream and other DRM programs through a virtual cable — it used to keep the previous mode's voice filters, which cut away the outer part of a DRM signal, so they never locked.
* **A smoother zoom slider.** The **Zoom** slider has 20 steps instead of 8, all zooming by the same factor, from the whole receiver at ×1 to a few kHz at ×20 — the old ×2 already jumped 64 times closer on a 30 MHz receiver.
* **Callsign-only searches.** The FT8, FT4 and JS8 map buttons, the WSPR log and the QRZ link in Additional Info search for the station's callsign alone, also when the site information shows the sysop as "Name, CALL"; an optional `"siteCallsign"` in `site_information.json` sets it explicitly.
* **A band propagation page.** **🌐 Propagation** in Additional Info opens the band conditions for the receiver's own location: every band from 160 m to 6 m graded Good, Fair, Poor or Closed for five distances, from the nearest ionosondes' live measurements, the position of the sun and the solar and geomagnetic data, on a 3D globe you can spin and tilt (or a flat map): band-reach rings for every open band, worked out direction by direction (skip zone and farthest reach, or one band filled on its own), the MUF and foF2 contours, the greyline and every ionosonde reporting now, worldwide. The server keeps a 10-minute copy of the ionosonde data at `/propagation.json`. See the [User Guide](https://github.com/sv1btl/PhantomSDR-Plus/blob/main/docs/USER_GUIDE.md#band-propagation).
* **Wider IQ: 24 and 48 kHz.** Besides the 12 kHz IQ, a station can offer 24 and 48 kHz: **Width** in the IQ Demodulators panel picks it, and the waterfall passband, the panel's picture, the demodulators and IQ recordings follow. 48 kHz takes in a whole group of broadcast stations or a CW/digital sub-band, and feeds Dream at 48 kHz through a virtual cable. It costs the station about 0.7 Mbit/s per listener at 24 kHz and 1.4 Mbit/s at 48 kHz, so it is off until the sysop adds `iq_wide = 48` (or 24) under `[server]`, with `iq_wide_listeners` (default 4) limiting how many listeners have it at once. It works with every receiver; KiwiSDR clients stay at 12 kHz. See the [User Guide](https://github.com/sv1btl/PhantomSDR-Plus/blob/main/docs/USER_GUIDE.md#wider-iq-24-and-48-khz).
* **A signal strength plot.** **Plotter** under Recording Options draws the S-meter's dBm reading as a running graph over the last 10, 30 or 60 seconds (**Fast / Medium / Slow**), with S-units and dBm scales, the peak of the span, a mark at every retune, pause and a PNG save — for watching fading or comparing antennas. See the [User Guide](https://github.com/sv1btl/PhantomSDR-Plus/blob/main/docs/USER_GUIDE.md#signal-strength-plot).
* **A tighter To Band.** **To Band** now leaves 5 kHz to spare at each band edge instead of 10 kHz, and so does everything that frames a band like To Band: **Tune** in the Users window, the decoders' frequency lists, a FreeDV Reporter click and a frequency link in the chat.
* **More FLDIGI modes: PSK63, PSK125, QPSK and Contestia.** The PSK31 variant has a **Mode** list — BPSK31, BPSK63, BPSK125, QPSK31, QPSK63 and QPSK125 — and the Olivia variant lists the Contestia configurations (8 / 250, 4 / 125, 4 / 250 and the rest) under its own heading. Every mode was checked against real recordings. Olivia and Contestia no longer print a burst of random characters when a transmission ends. Frequency markers `PSK63`, `PSK125`, `QPSK31`/`63`/`125` and `CONTESTIA` start the right decoder and sub-mode. See [docs/DECODERS.md](docs/DECODERS.md).
* **SERVICES: DSC, Time Signals and HF Selcall.** The **HFDL** button is now **SERVICES**, with a **Service** list in its window: **HFDL** as before; **DSC**, the GMDSS calls of ships and coast stations (distress, urgency, safety and routine, with MMSI country, position and working frequency); **Time Signals** from DCF77, MSF, RWM and WWV/WWVH — date, time, DUT1, the time in the receiver's own zone (from its QTH locator) and how late the minute mark reached the page; and **HF Selcall** (CCIR 493-4), the selective calls of Codan, Barrett and other HF networks. Frequency markers `DSC`, `TIME`, `DCF77`, `MSF`, `RWM`, `WWV`, `WWVH` and `SELCALL` start them. See [docs/DECODERS.md](docs/DECODERS.md).
* **Utility modes move to SERVICES.** **Maritime FSK / SITOR** and **Weather RTTY** left the FLDIGI window and are in the **SERVICES** list, between HFDL and DSC, with the same decoder and settings. **ALE 2G** is split in two: FLDIGI's **ALE 2G HAM** has the amateur HFLINK channels, and SERVICES' **ALE 2G (MIL-STD-188-141)** the government, military (MARS among them) and utility networks. `SITOR` and `RTTY-WX` markers open SERVICES; an `ALE` marker opens FLDIGI on an HFLINK channel and SERVICES on any other. The Users window shows them as SITOR, RTTY-WX and ALE.
* **Naval RTTY (PBB CARB) in SERVICES.** A new service for the Channel Availability / Receipt Broadcast of PBB, the Royal Netherlands Navy at Den Helder: RTTY at 75 baud, 850 Hz shift, ITA2, mark/space inverted, with a squelch and 21 PBB frequencies from 2474 to 20098 kHz. `RTTY-NAV` markers open it, and the Users window shows it as RTTY-NAV.
* **Screen-reader support for blind and low-vision listeners.** Every button, slider and list has a spoken name, and keyboard-opened pop-ups take and return the focus, with no change to the layout. **Alt + Shift + F** says the frequency, mode and bandwidth, **Alt + Ctrl** opens the number pad, **Alt + Shift + S** the signal (S-units, dBm, SNR), **Alt + Shift + R** and **Alt + Shift + C** read the decoders' text and new chat messages aloud, **Alt + Shift + H** lists the keys. Arrow keys no longer tune while a slider, list or text field has the focus. The /mobile page names its controls, reads the S-meter and says the new frequency. Works with NVDA, JAWS, Narrator, VoiceOver, TalkBack, Orca and ChromeVox. See [Screen Readers](docs/USER_GUIDE.md#screen-readers).
* **A schedule for spot reporting, and an automatic start.** The admin panel's Spot Reporting tab has a **Schedule**: every band and mode ticked for PSK Reporter or WSPRnet gets a row of 24 UTC hours to click or drag across, with **24h**, **Day**, **Night** (from the station's own locator), **Clear** and a button that gives the other modes of the band the same hours. Outside its hours a slot sleeps and frees its place on the receiver, **MAX SLOTS** counts the slots running at the same time hour by hour, and **SELECT ALL** ticks every band and mode. A saved change reaches the running decoder within seconds, without Stop/Start. **Start automatically after a reboot or crash** brings the decoder back by itself; after **STOP** it stays off, reboots included, until **START**. See [Spot Reporting](docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md#spot-reporting-autorun-ft8ft4js8wspr).
* **New in the FLDIGI window: ALE 2G, FST4W, MSK144 and PSK Browser.** **ALE 2G HAM** (MIL-STD-188-141), just before Packet in the variant list, lists the soundings, calls and messages of the 31 HFLINK amateur ALE channels; the other public network lists are in SERVICES. **FST4W** decodes the WSJT-X beacons of 2200 m, 630 m and 160 m, one UTC period of 2 to 30 minutes at a time, down to about −31 dB (a real 30-minute signal decoded at −44 dB). **MSK144** decodes WSJT-X meteor scatter pings on 6 m and 2 m. **PSK Browser** (the Browser — all signals knob under the PSK31 Mode list) decodes every BPSK31 signal in the passband at once, one line each; click one to follow it. **Olivia Browser** (Browser — all signals, under the Olivia Mode list) does the same for every Olivia or Contestia station of the chosen configuration, also below the noise. Markers `ALE`, `FST4W` and `MSK144` start them.
* **CW Browser.** Switch on the **Browser — all signals** knob in the CW window and every CW signal between 200 and 3000 Hz is decoded at once, one line each, with its frequency, speed and the station's callsign (CQ calls in green); a click hands a station to the normal CW decoder. The receiver is held on USB 200–3000 Hz while it runs and goes back to your own mode afterwards. In all three browsers (PSK, Olivia, CW) each line now keeps 4000 characters and gets its own scroll bar once it holds more than six lines, and **Save** writes every line, also the ones already dropped; the CW window has a **Save** button too. The keying sidebands of a strong station no longer show up as extra CW Browser lines.
* **Amateur RTTY Browser and MFSK Browser.** The **Browser — all signals** knob now also sits under **Shift** in Amateur RTTY and under **Mode** in MFSK16·32·64: every RTTY signal of the chosen shift and speed, or every MFSK signal of the chosen mode, between 300 and 2700 Hz is decoded at once, one line each, as in the PSK, Olivia and CW browsers, and a click on a line decodes only that station. Each RTTY station gets its own narrow filter, so a strong neighbour does not wipe out a weaker one; the MFSK Browser finds stations below the noise too and ignores the FT8 signals next door, which the decoder would otherwise print as nonsense. Every new control has hover text.
* **Video recordings show where you were tuned.** A video from Recording Options → Video carries the tuner's yellow marks: dotted yellow lines at the tuned frequency and the passband edges, through the spectrum and the waterfall, and the bracket across the top.
* **DRM panel with long locators.** A station with an 8- or 10-character grid locator now gets the "beamed at this station" group and the distance ranking in the DRM selector.
* **Documentation:** the Admin Panel, Several Receivers, Decoders, User Guide and Project Structure manuals cover all of the above in all seven languages, and the offline PDFs are rebuilt.

**What's new in v.5.4.0**

* **RADE V2 (beta).** A **RADE-v2U** button (mode RADE2) before RADE-v1L and RADE-v1U decodes RADE V2, the new waveform of FreeDV 3.0, on USB — on the station's server through FreeDV's rade_c, which `install_rade.sh` builds in `~/rade_c` next to RADE V1 in `~/radae`, at about 1 % of a CPU core per listener. V2 is still a beta at FreeDV. See the [RADE manual](docs/RADE_README.md#rade-v2-beta--rade2).
* **DRM in the Modes pop-up**, after IQ; RADE plays 6 dB lower so it no longer clips; `/mobile` gets a RADE2 button and loses its CW-L button.
* **RADE hears weaker signals.** RADE V2 keeps playing down to about −6 dB SNR when the signal looks like real V2 (it was muted below −2 dB); RADE V1 is resampled in one continuous stream like V2, which helps it most on fading signals; and the RADE2 filter is 1040–1930 Hz, so a strong neighbouring station no longer pulls V2 down. On test signals in noise and HF fading V2 now decodes about 2 dB weaker signals, and V1 gets about a quarter more of the speech through in fading. The RADE buttons read **RADE-v2U**, **RADE-v1L** and **RADE-v1U**, next to the **Modes** heading (was "Modes selector"). See the [RADE manual](docs/RADE_README.md#rade-v2-beta--rade2).
* **DRM decoder.** The **DRM** button decodes Digital Radio Mondiale on the station's server — one ubersdr-drm (the headless Dream decoder UberSDR uses) per listener, on zero-IF I/Q the server taps itself — so it plays in every browser, xHE-AAC included. A station selector lists the DRM Reception Project schedule with days, target area, listener confirmations and HFCC registrations, plus what this receiver has itself decoded in the last 30 days ("heard here" — also transmissions no schedule lists); with DRM on, the only sound is decoded DRM. On weak and fading signals a damaged frame fades into a short reverb and silence instead of a burst of noise, and the decoder resynchronises by itself (on a fading xHE-AAC broadcast: 515 s of programme in 539 s, 153 s before). New stations build it during installation; existing ones run `./setup-drm.sh` once, and again after an update. See the [decoder manual](docs/DECODERS.md#13-drm).
* **HFDL decoder.** The **HFDL** button decodes the messages aircraft exchange with ground stations on shortwave — dumphfdl in WebAssembly, ported from F1NSK's PhantomSDR-ORSAT — with FlightAware links and an aircraft map. See the [decoder manual](docs/DECODERS.md#12-hfdl).
* **Decoder buttons in two rows of six**, the drop-down in the same order; **HFDL**, **DRM** and **MORSE** (the CW decoder) markers start their decoders; AM with the synchronous detector reads **SAM** under the frequency.
* **Tune puts you on what another listener hears.** In the Users window opened from the receiver page, **Tune** copies the listener's frequency and mode, their filter bandwidth and their decoder.
* **Who runs which decoder.** The Users window (`users.html`) shows the decoder each listener is running — FT8, CW, DRM, HFDL… — next to the mode; the User Statistics (`stats.html`) get a **Decoder Popularity** panel and show the decoders in the longest sessions; the admin panel shows them on the Users page and as **Decoders** on the Visitors page.
* **Decoders checked and made more reliable.** FT8, FT4 and FT2 subtract each decoded station and decode the rest again, as WSJT-X does — on real-band recordings they now find 88 % of what WSJT-X finds (74 % before), a crowded 45-station FT8 slot 96 % (57 %) — with ordered-statistics decoding for weak FT8, and FT4/FT2 tolerate a clock up to about a second off. The CW decoder follows fading, keeps the first word, decides each letter on the signal itself (about half the wrong letters on weak signals) and has a **Pitch** slider (500–800 Hz; CW is now at 600 Hz everywhere). WSPR decodes busy bands with three passes and subtraction (15 stations in a slot: 89 % instead of 67 %). SSTV draws weak pictures to the end and recognises Robot 36 caught mid-picture. In the FLDIGI panel MFSK finds a mistuned signal by itself (up to about ±75 Hz), DominoEX stops printing noise after a transmission, and weather RTTY lets far less noise through. NAVTEX keeps its sync on weak signals (about twice the text at −6 dB). **Decoder ID** recognises narrow modes far below its old +10 dB limit — FT8 to about −5 dB, WSPR and CW to about −10 dB — and no longer names speech as CW. False decodes on pure noise stayed at zero throughout. See the [decoder manual](docs/DECODERS.md).
* **Receive diversity on the admin Dashboard.** A **Receive Diversity** card lists every listener combining your receiver with another one — PhantomSDR+, KiwiSDR, UberSDR or WebSDR — with the partner's address.
* **A deeper 3D view.** The 3D spectrum is now as tall as the flat spectrum and narrows towards the back, with lower rows further back; a click on an older row tunes to that row's frequency, and the **⚙** button sets the view's **Angle** and **Depth** (remembered in your browser).
* **IQ for listeners, as on a KiwiSDR or UberSDR.** An **IQ** button after FM sends 12 kHz of the band as raw I/Q — lossless, I left and Q right — with noise kept quiet and strong stations levelled. Recording in IQ saves a WAV that HDSDR, SDR#, SDR++ and SDRuno open already tuned (`PhantomSDR_<UTC>_<Hz>Hz_IQ.wav`), and Kiwi clients get it with `SET mod=iq` (`kiwirecorder.py -m iq`). An **IQ Demodulators** panel runs up to six receivers inside those 12 kHz in the listener's browser — each with its own mode (USB, LSB, CW, AM, DSB with carrier lock, FM), frequency, width, volume and pan, so both sides of a split can be heard at once, one in each ear. On by default; `iq = false` under `[server]` turns it off. See the [User Guide](docs/USER_GUIDE.md#iq-raw-iq).
* **SAM shows its carrier.** While SAM runs, `SAM +3.2 Hz · 97%` under the status indicators gives the station's carrier offset from the dial and how well the detector is locked.
* **Static IF Filters** offer 12.5 kHz in place of 4.5 kHz, and **To Band** leaves 10 kHz to spare at each band edge.

**What's new in v.5.3.0**

* **Your name or callsign, as on a WebSDR.** A box next to **Open Additional Info** takes your name or callsign — up to 10 characters, no spaces; left blank, you are anonymous plus a number. It shows in the Connected Users window and on its map, on your chat lines, and on your label under the other listeners' waterfall, followed by your location ("SV1BTL-Athens"); your own ★ carries it too. It is kept in your browser and follows you to the station's other receivers. On a phone the box sits above the frequency display. The chat panel no longer has a name field of its own.
* **Hover over a listener's label** under the waterfall to see the frequency and mode they are tuned to; click it to tune there.
* **No IP addresses in public.** As on a WebSDR, the public users list (`/users`, `users.json`) and the User Statistics no longer show any listener's IP address. The statistics count unique visitors by an anonymous code; the log files on disk keep the addresses for the sysop.
* **Admin panel:** the Users page shows each listener's name, location and IP address, and **⛔ Ban 24h** next to **⚡ Kick** disconnects a listener from every receiver and refuses them for 24 hours — kept across receiver restarts, with a list of active bans and an **Unban** button. The listener map now uses OpenStreetMap, like the APRS map.
* **A renewed Admin Panel.** **Alerts** by email and/or Telegram when a receiver goes off the air, comes back or is restarted, the thermal guard changes stage, or `crash.log` gets a new entry (Settings → Alerts, with a ✉ Send test). **Backup & restore** of the station's own settings — configs, `receivers.toml`, `station.conf`, site information, markers, band settings, relay, spot reporting, bans and every extra receiver — as one zip; a restore keeps each file it replaces in `admin-backups/`. A **Visitors** page with private statistics for 1 to 31 days: visitors, sessions, listening hours, most at once, busiest hours, countries, and the addresses with the most sessions at the same time, each with Ban 24h. A **System Health** card on the Dashboard: every receiver on air or off with its uptime, free disk and log folder sizes, the last `crash.log` entry. And **themes** — Phosphor green, Amber display, Metal panel, Modern dark, Light, and Auto (light from sunrise to sunset at the station's locator, dark at night) — picked with **THEME** in the top bar of every page. See [docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md](docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md).
* **The mouse wheel tunes anywhere in the black frequency box.** Over a digit it steps that digit as before; anywhere else in the box — the band name, the mode line, the clock — it tunes by the tuning step (Shift 1 kHz, Alt 10 kHz). Only the analog S-meter is left out, so the wheel over it scrolls the page.
* **Softer Noise Reduction.** NR now keeps about 1 dB more of the voice on weak signals and is more intelligible at every signal level — measured on 80 SSB test recordings with real band noise — for about 2 dB less noise removed between words.
* **The 3D view in the waterfall's exact colours.** The 3D spectrum no longer adds light and shade of its own: every point has exactly the colour the waterfall gives that level.
* **Decoders show the signal frequency.** While HF FAX, NAVTEX or the FLDIGI window is on, the frequency meter shows the station's own frequency — NAVTEX reads 518.000, not the 517.500 the receiver is tuned to in USB — and a frequency typed then is taken as the signal's. Decoding, the filter and the waterfall work exactly as before.
* **Decoder frequency lists re-checked.** The WeatherFAX, NAVTEX, maritime FSK (SITOR) and DWD weather RTTY frequencies and timetables were checked against NOAA, the IMO NAVTEX list, USCG NAVCEN and DWD on 22 October 2026, and are all current.
* **Documentation:** the User Guide (also the wheel in the frequency box and the NR figures), Admin Panel (also alerts, backup & restore, visitors, system health and themes), Connection Limits, Several Receivers and Project Structure manuals cover all of the above in all seven languages, and the offline PDFs are rebuilt.

**What's new in v.5.2.1**

* **An audio analyser.** The **📊 Show** button in the Spectrogram section now opens a full audio analyser: the demodulated audio with a dB grid and peak hold, an axis that follows the mode (reversed in LSB), the recommended bandwidths and your current filter, and an RF view of the station in AM and FM. Under it, the station's occupied width with a within / wide / splatter verdict, and in USB/LSB an SSB audio report — tonal balance against Standard, DX or ESSB, compression, mains hum, residual carrier and opposite sideband — with a curve to compare before and after and a listening report to download and send. In AM and FM an AM/FM report takes its place — modulation depth and sideband balance, FM deviation and 99 % bandwidth, carrier offset, audio bandwidth, CTCSS tone and hum. No zoom is needed: while it is open the analyser keeps its own fine-detail view of the station. You can tune and change the mode from its header, and its colours show how far each part of the spectrum stands above the noise. A Mute button lets you analyse your own transmission without feeding it back into the microphone. Adapted from PhantomSDR-ORSAT by F1NSK. See **[docs/USER_GUIDE.md](docs/USER_GUIDE.md)**.
* **Band buttons remember your frequency.** Coming back to a band tunes the last frequency you used there, with the band's zoom, brightness and contrast as before; a second click on the active band button goes back to the band's default. A frequency link in the chat now frames its band the way **To Band** does. See **[docs/USER_GUIDE.md](docs/USER_GUIDE.md)**.

**What's new in v.5.2.0**

* **Show All — the station's other receivers under your waterfall.** On a station with more than one receiver, a **Show All** checkbox after the receiver buttons puts each other receiver under the main waterfall as a live strip: its spectrum and waterfall, with its own markers and frequency scale, in its own colours and levels, the newest line at the top. Hover over it to read a frequency and the nearest marker; click and that receiver opens there, in the same tab — on a marker in the marker's mode. It is off until the listener ticks it, and costs about 10 KB/s per listener who does; each strip is a waterfall-only socket, so it is not counted as a listener on the other receiver, and the server needs no change. See **[docs/MULTI_RECEIVER.md](docs/MULTI_RECEIVER.md)**.
* **Back where you were.** Leaving a receiver's page remembers its zoom, brightness, contrast, mode and frequency. Its strip on the other page opens on that same part of the band, with a green ▼ where you were tuned and a **Full span** / **Last view** button, and clicking inside that view brings the receiver back exactly so — not at its whole range.
* **The right receiver by itself.** A frequency this receiver does not cover but another of the station's receivers does — typed, from a link, a bookmark, a DX spot, the chat or another listener's label — opens that receiver's page, tuned there. The mouse wheel, the digit steps and a rig connected over CAT never switch receivers.
* **A number pad, and a typed frequency brings its band.** Click the black frequency box and a number pad opens (or type on the keyboard; the two Enter keys pick kHz or MHz); click a digit to step it up, right-click to step it down. A typed frequency now lands the way that band's button would: the waterfall zoomed to the whole band, the band's brightness and contrast, and the band-plan mode — on this receiver or, through the automatic switch, on the one that covers it. See **[docs/USER_GUIDE.md](docs/USER_GUIDE.md)**.
* **Receive diversity switches even when only one site hears the station.** Until the two streams are aligned they are paired by arrival, and the better site is chosen by SNR as usual — so a station heard only by the remote now comes through, the case where diversity helps most. The panel shows **Ready (unaligned)** meanwhile. See **[docs/RECEIVE_DIVERSITY.md](docs/RECEIVE_DIVERSITY.md)**.
* **Connected Users and User Statistics per receiver.** With several receivers open side by side in one browser, each page's windows show its own receiver, from its own logs.
* **A bigger spectrum and waterfall on phones.** In the extended mobile view the spectrum is 1.6× taller and the waterfall nearly twice as tall as before (on a 412-pixel screen 57 + 42 pixels instead of 35 + 23); the desktop page is unchanged.
* **A 3D spectrum.** A **3D** button next to **Reverse** under Waterfall Controls turns the spectrum into a relief of the last few seconds — frequency across, time going back into the screen, strength as height — drawn by the listener's graphics card (WebGL2), so it costs the server nothing. It uses the waterfall's own colours, brightness, contrast and Auto Adj.; click to tune, drag and wheel to zoom work as on the flat spectrum; marks show seconds back and dB above the Min level; and a video recording made while it is open records it. Adapted from the 3D spectrum of [PhantomSDR-ORSAT](https://github.com/f1nsk40260/PhantomSDR-ORSAT) by F1NSK. See **[docs/USER_GUIDE.md](docs/USER_GUIDE.md)**.
* **A cursor line on the waterfall too.** The spectrum's dashed yellow line under the mouse now also shows on the waterfall, and hovering the waterfall draws it on the spectrum with its MHz readout.
* **Fixes:** the CW filter is centred on the 750 Hz tone (the tone used to sit 50 Hz from the filter's edge), and CW-L reaches the server as LSB; links written for KiwiSDR and UberSDR (`modulation=CWU`, `CW-U`, `CWL`) open in CW and CW-L; websdr.org registration works with sdr-list registration switched off, sends its ping over IPv4, and its callback (`/~~orgstatus`) now gets through `proxy.py`; the installers build the backend with OpenCL whenever the OpenCL step ran; with the waterfall switched off, the spectrum's tuning indicator follows a click again.
* **Documentation:** the User Guide (now with the 3D spectrum), Several Receivers, Receive Diversity and Project Structure manuals cover all of the above in all seven languages, and the offline PDFs are rebuilt.

**What's new in v.5.1.0**

* **An FLDIGI window with five more modes.** The decoder that used to be called RTTY is now **FLDIGI** — button and dropdown entry — and besides RTTY, SITOR, PSK31, Olivia, Packet and APRS it decodes **DominoEX**, **THOR**, **THROB / THROBX**, **Hellschreiber** and **MT63**, all in the listener's browser, with no fldigi installed anywhere. **MFSK16/32/64** have their own entry, right after Olivia. **New in v.3.9.0**

* **KiwiSDR client emulation — Kiwi clients can now connect to your receiver.** The server answers the KiwiSDR protocol alongside its own, on the same host and port you already publish, so **AetherSDR**, `kiwiclient` and other Kiwi software connect to a PhantomSDR-Plus receiver directly, with no proxy and no second port. It is a full session, not a listen-only tap: changing frequency, sideband or passband in the Kiwi client really retunes what PhantomSDR demodulates, and both the audio stream (`/kiwi/<id>/SND`) and the waterfall (`/kiwi/<id>/W/F`) are served. The S-meter reproduces what your web page actually displays, stage for stage — not just the power behind it, but the offset and range expansion the page puts on top — so a Kiwi client and the page show the same signal at the same strength, confirmed on a strong local station where both read −37 dBm. **It is off until you switch it on** — add `[kiwi_emulation]` with `enabled = true` to the `.toml` your receiver actually runs with and restart; leave it out and not one line of the bridge runs. The installers now offer it as a step of their own, and `./kiwi_install.sh` adds it to an existing tree at any time. One thing to know when you first listen: the bridge hands a Kiwi client exactly the samples a browser gets — measured identical — but the browser then rebuilds them with its own EQ and compressor, which a Kiwi client has not got, so the stream sounds thin by comparison. `audio_gain` (dB, Kiwi clients only) closes that gap, with a look-ahead peak limiter behind it so the level can actually reach the web page's without the 16-bit clamp flattening the peaks — 55 dB is where the two measured level with each other on a strong signal, and driving it to 70 dB did not clip a single sample in a quarter of a million. **The waterfall and the spectrum are on a real dB scale.** PhantomSDR stores each bin as a signed byte; a Kiwi client reads an unsigned one, so copying it straight across folded the scale back on itself and the display came out both saturated and upside down. Each bin is now converted to an absolute dBm before it is sent, so a carrier reads the **same level at every zoom** — what a genuine KiwiSDR gives you — and the spectrum agrees with the page's own calibrated S-meter to 0.1 dB. Frames are always 1024 bins now, instead of coming out short near the ends of your coverage and being drawn at the wrong scale, and the greeting carries centre frequency as well as bandwidth so clients can finally zoom all the way out. **The waterfall also runs at the rate the client asked for:** it was delivering 14 frames a second while announcing 23, which is what made the spectrum and waterfall look sluggish even though nothing was actually late — retuning measures 35–50 ms and the audio runs dead on real time. Kiwi clients now get the full rate, `SET wf_speed` is honoured, and `wf_fps_max` sets the ceiling. The web page's own waterfall keeps its cadence and no extra FFT work is done for either. Full section: **[docs/Aether_config.md](docs/Aether_config.md)**, in all seven languages.
* **SSTV no longer gives up during QSB or a burst of QRM.** The decoder was abandoning a picture part-way through on ordinary fades and short bursts of interference, clearing the mode and restarting the image from the top line. Three separate faults in the code that decides a lock is dead: the "was this ever a real signal" hit-rate test stayed switched on for the whole picture, although a genuine weak picture near 0 dB finds only about one sync pulse in nine — it now switches itself off once a lock has found five real pulses, so a false lock on noise still dies within about twenty lines. The backstop was a fixed count of forty missed lines, which is eight seconds on Martin M1 but only two and a half on Robot 36 — it is now twelve **seconds** of silence whatever the mode. And the sync detector only ever updated its comparison level when it found a pulse, so after a fade it was still measuring against the pre-fade signal and rejected exactly the weaker pulses that QSB gives you on the way back. A fade is now ridden out: the line timing free-runs across it, so the picture comes back **in register** — a band across the image and the rest carrying on where it belongs — while drawing stops after four seconds of genuine silence so a dropout no longer paints a screenful of static. Measured over ten runs each, a 2 s burst of QRM on Robot 36 broke the picture every time before and never now, and 3 s and 10 s fades that used to end the decode are ridden out; an 18 s fade still ends it, because by then the signal really is gone. Complete pictures on Martin M1, eight runs each: 0/8 → 2/8 at 0 dB, 1/8 → 3/8 at +1 dB, 3/8 → 5/8 at +2 dB, unchanged at +3 dB and above — better or the same at every level, with no change to how readily it locks onto something that is not there.
* **The installers gained a Kiwi step, and all four now point at systemd.** A fresh install can set the KiwiSDR bridge up during installation instead of afterwards; it is offered, not forced, and `PHANTOM_KIWI=y|n` covers unattended runs. Separately, the Arch, Fedora and openSUSE installers used to finish by telling you to start the admin panel with `./manage_admin.sh` — that starts a copy systemd does not know about, and with the units installed the two fight over port 3000. All four now print the `systemctl` form the Ubuntu installer already printed. A full unattended install of every supported distribution was then emulated in a container — Ubuntu 22.04/24.04/26.04, Debian 12/13, Arch, Fedora 44 and openSUSE Tumbleweed, with all four optional sub-installers enabled — and turned up four more bugs: `install-stats-server.sh` refused to run as root and stopped every `sudo ./install.sh` at step 15; the unattended final rebuild never ran on Arch, Fedora or openSUSE; RADE could not build on Fedora for want of libtool's m4 macros; and `procps` was in no dependency list anywhere, although `pgrep` is used by every launcher. All eight finish cleanly now. Container runs prove the software builds and starts, not that it works on air.
* **Nagle's algorithm is switched off on every connection.** `TCP_NODELAY` was set nowhere in the server, and every frame it sends is comfortably under one MSS — a 1040-byte waterfall line, a 1034-byte audio frame — which is exactly what Nagle holds back until the previous segment is acknowledged. Against a peer's delayed acknowledgement that is up to 40 ms of queueing added to a frame that was ready to leave. There was nothing to coalesce in the first place: these are whole messages, one per FFT hop. This removes a worst case rather than speeding up the normal one, so most listeners will notice nothing — and it cannot be measured on a local network at all, where acknowledgements come back instantly and Nagle never engages.
* **The admin panel can show the receiver's own log, and that log is a tenth the size.** `spectrumserver.log` was the one log the panel could not display; it now has a tab of its own beside LOGWEBSDR, and the viewer reads only the tail of a file, so log size no longer matters to it. Separately, 88% of that file turned out to be two loops reporting that nothing had happened — 13000 lines a day here, now 1400, with nothing diagnostic lost. The launchers also bound it every five minutes instead of only at startup, so a receiver left running for months finally has a ceiling on it.
* **The KiwiSDR bridge has a manual of its own.** [docs/Aether_config.md](docs/Aether_config.md), in all seven languages and in the offline PDFs — what the bridge does, installing it, a reference table for every `[kiwi_emulation]` key, connecting a client, audio level, the S-meter, the waterfall rate, and a table of symptoms with what to look at for each.

**New in v.3.7.0**

* **A CPU over-temperature guard now protects the machine on its own.** It watches the CPU temperature and responds in stages — a warning, then a lower CPU clock, then stopping the server, then restarting it once the machine has cooled — with each stage held for a sustain time so a brief spike during a compile never trips it. Nothing is hard-coded: the thresholds are derived from the trip points your own CPU publishes, landing near 95 °C on an Intel with a 100 °C limit, 90 °C on a Ryzen and 80 °C on a Raspberry Pi. It works with **any** start/stop method, because while the CPU is too hot it re-issues the stop on every 2-second check, so a watchdog, a systemd unit or a cron job that revives the server is undone until the machine is cool. **It ships inert** — the mode starts at `log`, recording in `crash.log` what it *would* have done, so you can watch your own machine for a week before letting it act. Full sysop manual: **[docs/THERMAL_GUARD.md](docs/THERMAL_GUARD.md)**, in all six translations.
* **The guard also runs without the admin panel.** `thermal_guard.py` is a standalone program with `--mode`, `--config` and `--once` flags — no JSON file is required to get protection — and `thermal-guard.service` is included for installations that do not use the panel.
* **The throttle stage no longer needs root.** `./setup-cpufreq-perms.sh` grants a `cpufreq` group write access to the CPU frequency ceiling and installs a `tmpfiles.d` rule so the permission survives a reboot, instead of running the panel — and every start/stop script it launches — as root. `--revoke` undoes it.
* **The admin panel and proxy can run under systemd.** Two ready units, `phantomsdr-admin.service` and `phantomsdr-proxy.service`, start both at boot and restart them within five seconds of a crash — which matters because the thermal guard lives inside the panel, so a panel that dies at 03:00 takes the protection with it. Installing the units is **strongly recommended** and is what `setup_admin.sh` now offers by default. `manage_admin.sh` remains fully supported for anyone who prefers to start things by hand, and for machines with no systemd at all; pick one method and stay with it, as both are described in [the setup guide](docs/ADMIN_PANEL_SETUP.md#step-3--start--stop--restart).
* **`setup_admin.sh` now sets up the whole panel, not just the ports.** It asks which scripts start and stop your receiver — the panel and the thermal guard both act through these — then how far the temperature guard may go on its own, and offers to grant the throttle permission and to install the two systemd units. It also installs `tomli-w`, which the panel needs to save `.toml` files.
* **The installers now set up the whole receiver on their own.** `install.sh` and the five distro variants install the **admin panel**, the **FreeDV RADE decoder** and the **statistics server** by default (answer `n` to skip any of them), install the **RX888 udev rules** when you pick that receiver, decide about **OpenCL from the hardware they find** instead of asking you to guess, patch the two **websocketpp headers** websdr.org registration needs, and finish with one full `recompile.sh`. Nothing on the machine has to be prepared by hand first — see [What the installer does](#what-the-installer-does--read-this-before-you-start).
* **The RADE installer runs on any of the four package managers.** It installs its dependencies through apt, pacman, dnf or zypper — whichever the machine has — falling back to pip for a module a distribution does not ship, and it upgrades `websockets` itself when the distro version is older than the 11.0 the sidecar needs. It also still picks the right variant for your system: Ubuntu 22.04 needs `install_rade_ubuntu22.sh`, and each script reads `/etc/os-release` and offers to hand over to the other when it does not match.
* **A new `smeter_theme.sh` tool** sets which analog S-meter face is the default for new visitors, and offers to recompile the frontend for you afterwards.
* **The magic-eye indicator now behaves like a real EM84 tube**, following the F1NSK design more closely.
* **Mobile fixes.** The `/mobile` page follows the band plan as you tune, its digital S-meter reads the same level as the desktop one, and the meter gained an extra segment.

**New in v.3.6.1**

* **The S-meter / layout variants now switch without reloading the page.** The ⚙️ menu at the top right used to navigate to a separate build of the site (`/analog`, `/digital`, `/v2-analog`, `/v2-digital`), so every switch reloaded everything: the audio stopped, the waterfall cleared and any running decoder was lost. Since the four `App__*_smeter_.svelte` files were merged into one `App.svelte` those "versions" are just two properties of the same page, so the menu now changes them **in place** — the switch is silent and listening continues uninterrupted. Each visitor's choice is remembered in their own browser, and the variant picked in `./recompile.sh` is the **starting** variant a first-time visitor sees.
* **One desktop build instead of five.** With the variants switchable at runtime, the four extra builds were four identical copies of the same application, so they are gone: `build-all.sh` now produces the desktop page and `/mobile`, and a full rebuild takes **about half as long again** (roughly 29 s against 53 s here). The per-variant scripts `build-analog.sh`, `build-digital.sh`, `build-v2-analog.sh`, `build-v2-digital.sh` and the obsolete `switch-version.sh` have been removed, and the `./recompile.sh` build menu is now simply *all* / *desktop only* / *mobile only*. Old bookmarks to the four retired URLs are not broken — each keeps a small page that redirects to `/`.
* **The simplified mobile page now follows the band plan as you tune.** On `/mobile` the mode used to change only when you pressed a band button — typing a frequency, or stepping to one, kept whatever mode was selected, so a QSY from 40 m to a broadcast station stayed in LSB. The mode now follows `bands-config.js` whenever the dial moves into a different band or a different mode segment, exactly as the full interface has always done. A mode you choose by hand still sticks while you move about inside that segment; outside every defined band the mode is left alone; and while **RADEL/RADEU** are running they keep the receiver, so tuning does not drop the RADE decoder back into a listening mode.
* **Your frequency now follows you between the two mobile views.** `/mobile` and the extended mobile view are separate pages, so switching between them reloaded the site and dropped you back on the receiver's default frequency. Both switch buttons now carry the current frequency and mode in the link, and both pages read it on arrival, so you stay on the signal you were listening to. The address bar keeps up with your tuning as well, which means reloading the page, bookmarking it, or sending the link to someone else all return to that exact frequency. The **mode follows the band plan in `bands-config.js`**: your own mode travels with you, but where the other view has no equivalent for it the band plan decides — a broadcast frequency arrives in AM, 40 m in LSB, a CW segment in CW. `SAM` on `/mobile` maps to AM with the synchronous detector in the extended view and back again, and a frequency outside the receiver's coverage is pulled back to the nearest edge.

A running decoder now keeps the mode and passband it needs across retunes, including a jump to another band, and the band's own mode returns when the decoder is switched off. Mode buttons still win — a deliberate choice by the operator is never overridden — and the CW decoder is unaffected, since it decodes in whatever mode you are listening in.

Bands where two conventions are in use (40, 80, 160 and 10 m) list both windows. While a window is tuned the grabber holds the receiver the same way a decoder does, and the passband follows the dial as you hunt along the band. See the [decoder documentation](docs/DECODERS.md).

Add `-q` for the previous two-line output. The **RADE sidecar is now built into the start scripts** — it starts once the server is confirmed running, is restarted on its own if it exits (without restarting the server), is stopped by `stop-websdr.sh`, and can be skipped with `RADE_ENABLED=0`; where RADE is not installed the server starts normally and the summary just says the sidecar was not activated. Finally, `spectrumserver`'s own output — which was previously discarded — is kept in **`spectrumserver.log`** (rotating at 10 MB), with the `[WebSDROrg]` lines also copied into `logwebsdr.txt` — minus the routine `/~~orgstatus` and keep-alive-ping chatter, so what you read there is the registration story and any errors, not a running tally.
* The Admin Panel's Spot Reporting page now keeps **two separate counters**. The **SPOTS UPLOADED PER DECODER** tiles (FT8 / FT4 / WSPR) count uploads **since the daemon last started**, with the decode count and queue depth under each; a decoder whose destination is switched off shows `reporting off` instead of a bare `0`. The number beside each **BANDS & MODES** checkbox is that band+mode's **all-time** upload total, stored in `autorun-totals.json` so it survives restarts — a leading dot (`·123`) means the slot has decoded but not yet uploaded.

**New in v. 3.5.0**

- **Automatic spot reporting to PSK Reporter and WSPRnet** — a built-in *autorun* engine decodes signals off-air locally and uploads your spots to the reporting networks, with no extra software required:
  - **FT8 / FT4 → [PSK Reporter](https://pskreporter.info/)**, over the native IPFIX/UDP protocol, with spots batched and flushed on the recommended ≥5-minute cycle,
  - **WSPR → [WSPRnet](https://wsprnet.org/)**, via the standard upload endpoint (WSPR is sent *only* to WSPRnet, to avoid duplicate spots),
  - Runs several band/mode slots at once (e.g. `20m:ft8`, `40m:ft8`), independently of connected users,
  - Each network can be enabled or disabled separately, and your identity (callsign + Maidenhead grid) is taken from your site configuration,
  - A **dry-run** mode logs the spots it *would* send without actually uploading them, for safe testing.
- **All decoders moved off the main thread** — SSTV, HF FAX, NAVTEX, FSK/RTTY and CW each run in their own Web Worker, so decoding no longer competes with audio playback:
  - No audio gap or dropout when a decoder is switched on or off,
  - The waterfall and GUI stay smooth while a picture or page is decoding,
  - NAVTEX and FSK/RTTY can now run at the same time (they previously shared one set of internal state),
  - FAX no longer discards your LPM / IOC / shift selection when the decoder is activated,
  - HF FAX and NAVTEX now switch the receiver to USB *before* starting, so the retune is never fed into the decoder, and SSTV picks the sideband from the band (LSB below 10 MHz, USB above) while still honouring a manual change.
- **SSTV no longer triggers on noise** — a static crash, a spark or mains hash used to be enough to start a decode. Every detection gate now has an absolute reference: a tonality test on the sync and VIS tones, a confirmation pass that must predict the next lines before anything is drawn, and a signal-presence gate. Forced modes are verified the same way instead of painting noise immediately.
